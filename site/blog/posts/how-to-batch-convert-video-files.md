# How to Batch Convert Video Files with EncodeX

Converting videos is one of those chores that's fine once and painful twenty times. Open a file, pick a format, wait for the progress bar, open the next file, pick the same format, wait again. If you've ever stared at a phone's camera roll full of clips you need to send, or a downloads folder full of videos in the wrong format, you already know how tedious it gets.

Batch conversion fixes all of that. With EncodeX, you can convert multiple videos at once: drag an entire folder into the batch queue, choose an output format once, and walk away while EncodeX works through the list for you — up to four jobs running in parallel at the same time.

## Why Batch Convert Video Files?

The main reason to convert videos in a batch is time. Converting files one by one means repeating the same clicks over and over, and babysitting every progress bar until it finishes. A folder of twenty clips becomes a small afternoon project.

A batch video converter changes that in three ways:

- **One setup, many files.** You choose the output format and settings once, and every file uses the same configuration. No re-picking MP4 twenty times.
- **No babysitting.** You start the queue and walk away — make coffee, answer emails, or run the batch overnight.
- **Consistent output.** Because every file uses the same preset, you don't end up with a mix of different codecs, resolutions, and bitrates.

On top of that, EncodeX runs the batch queue with **up to four parallel jobs**, so a folder of twenty videos finishes in a fraction of the time a one-at-a-time converter would take.

## How to Batch Convert Video Files with EncodeX

The process takes about four steps. Here's the whole thing.

### 1. Download and Open EncodeX

[Download EncodeX for free](/download), install it, and open it. There's no account, no sign-up, and no watermark on your output files.

### 2. Drag In Your Files

This is the satisfying part. Grab a folder — or a selection of files — and **drag and drop** it straight into the EncodeX window. You can drop in 5 files or 200; both work the same way. EncodeX reads each file instantly, showing you the resolution, codec, and duration for each one.

### 3. Choose Your Output Format

Pick a preset — say **MP4 with H.264**, the most compatible format for phones, TVs, and uploads. The [video converter](/video-converter) page walks through every format and preset if you want to compare options first.

### 4. Start the Batch

Click start (or **Convert All**), and the queue begins. EncodeX processes up to **four videos at the same time**, each with its own progress bar. Done files are marked as they finish, and you can add new files to the queue while it's still running.

That's it. The whole folder converts with the settings you picked in seconds of setup.

## Tips for Large Batches

Handling a couple hundred files works the same as handling a dozen, but a few habits make big batches smoother:

- **Let the parallel queue do the work.** EncodeX runs up to four jobs at once, so resist the urge to start files individually. One queue, four at a time, everything else waiting in line.
- **Use pause and resume.** Need your CPU for something else for a minute? Pause the queue, do your thing, resume where you left off. Nothing is lost or restarted from scratch.
- **Reorder with drag and drop.** The files you need first can be pulled to the front of the queue at any time, even while others are running.
- **Turn on hardware acceleration.** EncodeX uses your GPU (NVENC, QSV, AMF, VideoToolbox) automatically where available, which makes large batches dramatically faster. See how it works in the [hardware acceleration guide](/blog/posts/gpu-hardware-acceleration-guide).
- **Set a clear output folder.** Choose a destination up front so all converted files land in one place you can find again.
- **Run it overnight.** For very large folders, you can even configure EncodeX to shut down your computer when the batch is finished.

## Common Use Cases for Batch Conversion

Batch conversion isn't a niche feature — it's the answer to several everyday situations:

- **Phone footage to MP4.** A vacation's worth of clips from your phone, converted and ready to share or archive in one pass.
- **Screen recordings.** Compress a semester of lecture recordings, meetings, or gameplay captures so they take up far less space.
- **Cleaning up downloads.** That folders worth of MKV, AVI, and WebM files your player won't open, converted to a format everything plays.
- **Camera exports.** Raw camera exports to a uniform, editable format with identical settings across the whole shoot.

Whatever the source, the workflow is the same — drop it in, pick a format, and let the queue do the rest.

## FAQ

### How many videos can I batch convert at once with EncodeX?

As many as you want to add — dozens or even hundreds of files in one go. EncodeX processes up to four of them at the same time through its parallel queue, so even large folders finish quickly while the rest wait their turn.

### Can I pause or resume a batch conversion?

Yes. You can pause and resume the entire queue at any point, add files while it's running, cancel individual jobs, and drag to reorder what converts next.

### Will batch converting reduce my video quality?

No. Batch conversion uses exactly the same settings as converting a single file. Choose a sensible preset like MP4, and the output quality matches what you'd get converting the same file on its own.
