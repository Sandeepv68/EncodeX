# How to Extract MP3 from a Video (3 Easy Steps)

Maybe you recorded a lecture in video and only need the audio. Maybe it's the soundtrack from a concert video, or an interview you want to turn into a podcast episode. Either way, the fastest way to get a clean MP3 from a video is EncodeX — a free, open-source FFmpeg GUI that runs entirely on your computer.

## Why MP3?

Because MP3 is the universal format. Your phone plays it, your car plays it, your old MP3 player plays it. It compresses a full audio track into a small file, and at 320 kbps the quality is practically indistinguishable from the original to most ears. It's the safest choice for anything you want to carry around.

## What You'll Need

- EncodeX — free download for Windows, macOS, or Linux
- A video file (MP4, MKV, MOV, AVI, WebM, FLV, WMV, and more all work)
- About 30 seconds

## Step 2: Drop In Your Video

Drag your video file into the window. EncodeX reads it instantly and shows you the details — length, video codec, audio track, and more.

## Step 3: Extract the Audio as MP3

Choose **Extract Audio**, pick **MP3**, and click **Convert**. Done. You get a clean MP3 on your computer within moments.

Want to do more than one? Drag in a whole folder of videos and EncodeX will extract an MP3 from each one, all at once.

## Understanding Sound Quality

MP3 is "lossy" — it removes some audio data to save space. The bitrate setting controls that trade-off:

- **320 kbps** — highest MP3 quality, nearly indistinguishable from the original
- **256 kbps** — excellent quality, slightly smaller files
- **192 kbps** — good for podcasts and voice recordings
- **128 kbps** — small files, fine for speech, audible loss on music

If you want the absolute original sound, export to **WAV** (uncompressed, huge files) or **FLAC** (lossless, much smaller than WAV). Both are one click in EncodeX — see the full [audio extraction guide](/extract-audio-from-video) for details.
