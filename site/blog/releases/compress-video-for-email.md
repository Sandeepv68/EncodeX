# How to Compress a Video for Email in 2026

Ever tried to send a video by email, only to hit the "file too large" error? Most providers cap attachments around 25 MB. A single phone video is often 10 to 50 times that. The fix is simple: **compress the video** before you hit send.

## Why Videos Are Too Big for Email

Video files are big because of high resolution, high frame rates, and inefficient default codecs. Your phone might record casually, but it saves at a high bitrate. A 1-minute 4K clip can easily reach hundreds of megabytes — far over the email limit.

The good news: you usually don't need all that data. With the right compression, a video can shrink to a fraction of its size while looking nearly identical to the human eye.

## Key Tips for Email-Ready Videos

- **Shorter is better** — there's rarely a reason to send a 10-minute clip. Trim it first.
- **Match it to the screen** — most people watch on phones; you rarely need more than 1080p.
- **Use a modern codec** — outputting to [H.265/HEVC](/codecs/h265) can roughly halve file size at similar quality compared to H.264, though [H.264](/codecs/h264) remains the safest universal choice.
- **Batch compress** — have a folder of clips? EncodeX can also [compress videos in batch](/video-compressor) while you do something else.

## What About Online Compressors?

Web tools seem easy, but they mean uploading your video to a stranger's server, dealing with size caps, waiting through upload/download times, and often getting a watermark on the output. With EncodeX everything happens on your machine — private, fast, and free forever.
